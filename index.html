What we offer

AI integration is the flagship — and the cherry on top.

Everyone's pitching "AI integration" right now because it's the trend, and to be fair, it's most of what we do. But it's the cherry on top: roughly 80% of the work is data science and engineering — understanding what data you actually have, finding where the value in it is, and building the foundation that makes it usable. The AI layer sits on top of that foundation, for efficiency. Skip the foundation and the AI layer has nothing solid to stand on.

How we build

We don't build generic. We build bespoke.

Off-the-shelf tools solve the problems every business shares — but to fit everyone, they have to stay generic, and it's too expensive for them to customise for every need. That gap is where we sit: those tools end up siloed, disconnected from each other, and we fill the gap by integrating them together. Three shapes that takes:

i.

Pulling fragmented tools into one thing

Day-to-day work spread across too many disconnected tools, stitched together by hand — we build the thing that ties it together.

Example — MIHE

A higher-education institute running Moodle. Building a single week of course content meant a tutor manually working across NotebookLM, ChatGPT, PowerPoint and Claude — uploading and downloading between each one — with no way to see what students were actually doing on the platform, or whether tutors' content was hitting the institute's own standard.

We built a bespoke system tailored to that exact workflow: the engagement metrics a platform like Canvas gives natively, most of the content-authoring pain points automated with AI, and visibility into both sides — tutor output and student engagement — in one place.

ii.

Bespoke analytics over generic dashboards

A standard dashboard tells you what moved. It rarely tells you why — and why is where the decision actually lives.

Example — Hilti

A global tooling and construction-supply company. Their global team gets customer-service analytics, but the frontline team had no way to know why a signal moved — was it one employee, the product itself, or something else entirely on the customer's side. We built root-cause analysis that attributes the "why," not just the "what."

Example — PING

A golf equipment company sitting on a large body of textual customer and product data, with no mainstream analytics vendor able to surface signal from it. We're helping mine that data for the signals their R&D team needs to design better clubs.

Example — Sutton Hill Farm Auctions

A family-run antiques and collectables auction house selling through the Easy Live Auction platform. The platform logs raw results — lot, estimate, hammer price — but never says why a lot didn't sell. We built a dashboard reading her own six years of sale history: real sell-through and category patterns, whether pricing sat outside the norm for that category, and a valuation assistant grounded in her actual comparable past lots.

iii.

Bespoke point-solutions for a specific pain point

The narrowest kind — one very specific, very annoying manual process, solved end to end. Usually involves AI somewhere in the middle.

Example — SA Law Practice (immigration advisers)

Advisers were manually pulling case documents from SharePoint, reading through passports, certificates and bank statements by hand, then re-typing the relevant fields into the government's immigration portal one page at a time. We built a pipeline that extracts the fields automatically and pre-fills the portal form inside the adviser's own logged-in session — one page at a time, with the adviser reviewing and confirming each one before moving on, since they carry professional liability for what gets submitted under the firm's name.

Pricing

Worth knowing going in

Individual council planning portals are free, but checking each one by one doesn't scale by hand. Rather than paying an ongoing subscription to an aggregator like Searchland or LandHawk, we can build and run that ingestion directly — same coverage, no third-party licence fee.

Scoring model

Weighted rules to start

A simple weighted rule-based score — not full ML — is the right starting point: transparent, easy for Dalycom's team to sanity-check and adjust, and it doesn't need training data to get going. It can get more sophisticated from here — once there's enough real outcome data to learn from, the same pipeline can move to a more intelligent, more automated scoring model.

Beyond the pipeline

Bespoke lead management, not what a generic tool gives you

Scoring and ranking leads is only the first half. Off-the-shelf CRMs manage a lead the same generic way regardless of what kind of business is selling — a stage pipeline and a follow-up reminder. A bespoke layer can manage the lead the way Dalycom's team actually sells: routing a CCTV-signal lead to whoever handles CCTV, a cloud-migration signal to whoever handles Cloud, with follow-up timing and messaging built around each service line rather than one generic sequence applied to everything.

AI opportunity

Three places the market's already heading

Not theory — we can see big businesses already moving this way, and each one maps onto something Dalycom already runs.

i.

Agents that resolve things, not just route them

This goes a step further than the ticket-triage idea already on the table. Instead of just routing a ticket to a person, the agent works out what actually needs doing and does it.

Live precedent — BT

A separate BT team already runs this: a customer query comes in, an agent works out what tasks it actually maps to, then launches agents directly against the network and underlying infrastructure to resolve it — collapsing a multi-step manual process into one automated pass.

ii.

Chatbot cost & performance layer

Running a chatbot isn't free, and "is it actually working" is a different question from "is it switched on." Both need measuring, not assuming.

Live precedent — BT

Another BT team is focused on exactly this: understanding what their chatbot actually costs to run, where that cost can be trimmed, and separately, how well it's actually performing.

Relevant to Dalycom specifically: Omnichat already ships chatbot automation for your clients' WhatsApp. This is the layer that tells you whether it's earning its keep — not something Omnichat itself reports on.

iii.

Cloud usage, cost & prediction layer

The same shape of idea already sitting on the Cloud Based Services line of the opportunity map — usage/cost anomaly detection, flagging accounts that are over- or under-provisioned, predicting cost before it lands.

Live precedent — Cloud Bridge & BT

Comparable work already built for Cloud Bridge and BT: an agentic/analytics layer that manages cloud accounts, flags provisioning anomalies, and predicts cost trends — not a new idea for AYD, existing precedent behind it.
